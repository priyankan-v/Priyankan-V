# A Strange Journey of a Person,

Who secured a **top island rank in the highly competitive national advanced-level examination**, which earned him admission to **the country’s leading technological university to study engineering**.  

After the first semester’s competitive selection, he was among the **small group admitted to one of the most demanding specializations in electronics and communication engineering**.

Like many students, he followed the advice that is repeated everywhere:

> **Study hard. Compete well. Choose a challenging field.**

But unlike many others, he wanted to explore **different areas of technology**, rather than simply following the common path into software development or machine learning.

So he developed a strong interest in **Embedded Systems and Robotics**.

---

## Where the Journey Becomes Interesting

Locally, there are **almost no opportunities in these areas**.

The common advice is simple:

> *"Apply abroad."*

That sounds reasonable — until most positions require:

> **“2+ years of experience in relevant area.”**

Which raises a simple question:

> **In a country where these opportunities barely exist, where exactly is that experience supposed to come from?**

---

## The Applications

So he started applying for **Research Assistant** and **Research Engineer** roles.

Many applications were sent.  
Most responses were… **silence**.

---

## The Next Attempt

Next, he explored **Master’s programs**.

Scholarships are limited, and he now finds himself in a situation where **even paying for IELTS and application fees is financially difficult**.

Then he looked into **PhD opportunities**.

Many positions require **publications and prior research experience** — things that are usually gained through positions that, once again, rarely respond.

---

## The Advice Everyone Gives

People often say:

> **“Do the work you love.”**

But in a situation like this, a quiet question appears:

> **How exactly is someone supposed to do the work they love, when the path to even begin that work barely exists?**

---

## The Strange Conclusion

After doing everything students are told to do:

- Study hard  
- Rank high  
- Enter a competitive engineering program  
- Choose a demanding specialization  

He arrives at a rather strange conclusion:

> **The system works perfectly — as long as you already have the opportunities it requires.**

---

## An Unfinished Thought

And somewhere along the way, another thought slowly begins to appear.

About **giving up on everything and ending ...**

About **quitting everything and simply walking away from the pursuit altogether**.

And the story pauses there —  
with a question that still has no clear answer.
